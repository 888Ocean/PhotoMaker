# Temporal Tide

Temporal Tide treats a photograph as a place where several moments may occupy the same air. Space remains continuous and believable while time folds quietly inside it; repetition appears as an afterimage rather than a grid. The first glance should encounter a calm landscape, and the second should discover that the landscape is holding more than one present.

Scale carries the narrative. A distant figure is almost a mark, a nearer figure becomes a body, and the nearest becomes an event. These repetitions form a diagonal current through the image, meticulously balanced so they feel pulled by the same invisible tide rather than arranged by a layout system.

Color belongs to one atmosphere: bleached cyan sky, mineral blue water, and small amber vessels. Each imported fragment is painstakingly matched to its surrounding water; edges dissolve into texture instead of declaring collage. A faint chromatic echo may appear around motion, but only as an optical trace, executed with master-level restraint.

The composition is full-bleed and cinematic. Empty sky is not decorative whitespace but duration—an immense pause above the compressed human movement below. The horizon anchors the work while the changing bodies disturb it just enough to make stillness perceptible. Every transition should look as though it was refined over countless hours by an expert image-maker.

Typography is a whisper embedded in the atmosphere, never a title block. One brief phrase may float inside the sky and one small location mark may sit near the edge. The work communicates through scale, recurrence, and disappearance; language only confirms what the eye has already begun to feel.
