# Tidal Notation

Tidal Notation treats open space as an active field rather than an absence. Broad, luminous intervals hold small, exact forms in suspension, allowing distance to become a visible material. Every margin, horizon, and pause is meticulously calibrated so quietness feels intentional and structurally alive.

Color behaves like measured weather: one dominant atmospheric family, one mineral neutral, and a single warm signal. Tonal transitions carry depth while the accent appears only where attention must change direction. The palette should feel painstakingly tuned by a master colorist, with no hue present merely for decoration.

Repetition becomes notation. Slender verticals, brief dashes, numbered observations, and widely spaced markers establish a patient rhythm across the field. Irregular human traces interrupt this order at a smaller scale, producing tension without spectacle; the composition rewards prolonged looking through countless carefully judged relationships.

Photography remains materially honest and visually sovereign. Cropping reveals changes in scale and time without disguising the source, while analytical marks touch the image lightly, as if recording rather than explaining it. Each boundary, crop, and registration point must show master-level execution and deep respect for the photographed moment.

Typography is sparse, precise, and treated as another spatial instrument. A lyrical serif gives the atmosphere a voice; a disciplined monospaced face supplies quiet coordinates and labels. Words never narrate what the image already knows. The finished object should feel meticulously crafted over countless hours: calm from a distance, exacting and richly observed up close.
